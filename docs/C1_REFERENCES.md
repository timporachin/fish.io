# C1 largemouth bass anatomical references

Research checked 2026-10-12 for a largemouth bass reference model (*Micropterus salmoides*, the name used in the cited profiles). The facts below support a recognizable adult fish silhouette; coloration and markings vary with age, water, and condition.

## Supported modeling cues

- **Head and mouth:** The large, sloping mouth has a maxilla/upper jaw that reaches behind the eye; the lower jaw is wide and slightly longer than the upper jaw. These are strong silhouette cues. South Carolina DNR and Fisheries and Oceans Canada (DFO) describe the jaw passing the eye; DFO also states the relative jaw lengths. [SCDNR species profile](https://www.dnr.sc.gov/fish/species/largemouthbass.html) · [DFO identifying features and photo gallery](https://www.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/species-especes/profiles-profils/largemouthbass-achigangrandebouche-eng.html)
- **Trunk:** Moderately laterally compressed, elongated yet robust/streamlined body; dorsal head profile slightly convex. Minnesota DNR calls the body a laterally flattened football. Avoid a cylindrical or highly deep-bodied outline. [Smithsonian NEMESIS species summary](https://invasions.si.edu/nemesis/species_summary/168160) · [Minnesota DNR profile](https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/minnaqua/speciesprofile/largemouthbass.html)
- **Dorsal:** One connected dorsal fin with distinct spinous and soft-rayed sections, deeply notched so the sections nearly appear separate. [SCDNR](https://www.dnr.sc.gov/fish/species/largemouthbass.html) · [DFO](https://www.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/species-especes/profiles-profils/largemouthbass-achigangrandebouche-eng.html)
- **Fin counts:** Sources vary by reference and count convention. USGS NAS gives 9–11 dorsal spines; Smithsonian NEMESIS gives 9–12 spines and 11–14 soft rays; FishBase's field guide gives total counts of 10 spines and 11–14 rays, while its descriptive passage says 9–10 spines and 12–13 soft rays. A practical model target is 9–11 spines and 12–14 soft rays; exact ray geometry can be simplified for a small real-time asset. [USGS NAS species profile](https://nas.er.usgs.gov/queries/factsheet.aspx?SpeciesID=401) · [Smithsonian NEMESIS](https://invasions.si.edu/nemesis/species_summary/168160) · [FishBase field guide](https://www.fishbase.se/Fieldguide/FieldGuideSummary.php?c_code=620&genusname=Micropterus&speciesname=salmoides)
- **Paired fins and tail:** Pelvic fins are thoracic (set on the underside below the forward body); DFO describes the pectorals as amber and clear. Tail is shallowly forked per Smithsonian NEMESIS; FishBase describes the caudal outline as emarginate or rounded. This supports a shallow central notch rather than a deeply forked or lunate tail. The consulted sources do not specify exact pectoral/pelvic fin proportions. [Smithsonian NEMESIS](https://invasions.si.edu/nemesis/species_summary/168160) · [DFO](https://www.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/species-especes/profiles-profils/largemouthbass-achigangrandebouche-eng.html) · [FishBase](https://www.fishbase.se/Fieldguide/FieldGuideSummary.php?c_code=620&genusname=Micropterus&speciesname=salmoides)
- **Color and pattern:** Green/olive or brassy-green dorsum, lighter green/olive sides, pale white to yellow underside. Dark olive mottling on the back and a dark lateral band, often broken into blotches, runs from the operculum/gill area toward the caudal base. Bars/stripe are more apparent in juveniles; pectoral fins may be amber and clear. The body can become darker and more contrasted in clear weedy water, and paler green in cloudy water. [USGS NAS](https://nas.er.usgs.gov/queries/factsheet.aspx?SpeciesID=401) · [SCDNR](https://www.dnr.sc.gov/fish/species/largemouthbass.html) · [DFO](https://www.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/species-especes/profiles-profils/largemouthbass-achigangrandebouche-eng.html) · [FishBase](https://www.fishbase.se/Fieldguide/FieldGuideSummary.php?c_code=620&genusname=Micropterus&speciesname=salmoides)
