## Reconstruction cues from inspected pixels

- In the **closed side view**, the snout is a short, blunt dome that rolls into an upper lip with visible rounded thickness. A soft curved seam runs backward from the front of the snout and returns into the cheek below/near the eye. The dark seam is a separation between fleshy volumes, not a long black painted line or a perfectly level slot. The posterior maxillary/cheek surface reaches toward or beyond the rear eye margin, consistent with the DFO diagnostic text. Place the side mouth corner deep enough in the head that the slit cannot read as a tiny frontal notch.
- The **lower mandible** is a continuous broad volume, slightly forward of the upper tip at rest, with a rounded, full ventral chin. Its lower contour rises back into the cheek/gill region without a straight planar edge. The front image shows that the lip rim is broad across the face and wraps around the projecting lower jaw; the chin is deep under the upper muzzle rather than a thin shelf. Build both sides of this rim in actual geometry so frontal and three-quarter views retain volume.
- The **maxillary side plate** below the eye is an oblong, softly edged overlapping form. It should visually connect the backward-running mouth seam to the cheek. Do not fuse it into a rigid-looking triangular wedge or a single flat strip. The eye remains a separate recessed round feature above it.
- In the **open three-quarter photograph**, the upper lip rolls into the snout as a thick fleshy rim. The maxillary side plate curves down and back from the upper muzzle toward the cheek below the eye, forming a long side wall at the back of the opening. The lower mandible is a substantial tapering mass: a thick dark rounded anterior lip continues into pale side walls and a bulky chin, rather than becoming a detached flat strip. There is an actual recessed oral cavity with visible interior surfaces and depth between the jaws. The posterior mouth opening disappears into the fleshy cheek below the eye. Perspective, the hand on the lip and fishing tackle mean the photo supports these forms, not exact joint angles or dimensions.
- If an **open pose** is needed, depress the whole lower-jaw mass from a posterior hinge, let the upper/premaxillary rim move slightly forward and the maxilla rotate, and open a cavity with depth and lateral width. This is a modeling inference from the feeding studies, not a measured rotation angle. A modest ~25° mouth-opening validation pose can check that both lips, the mouth corner and interior stay volumetric from side, three-quarter and front views. The hand-held extreme in Stock 482505893 is not the relaxed default or a target for that angle; reserve a very wide gape for an intentional feeding animation.

## Evidence boundary

Stock 482505893 now supplies a pixel-inspected, species-identified open-mouth photo, though its age is unverified and the jaw is physically pulled open. Its wide gape demonstrates structural depth, not natural resting shape. The PNAS figure independently supplies a species-verified feeding sequence through its caption, while the Minden record supplies an explicitly open-mouth wildlife photo link; neither external image's pixels were directly inspected here. Do not present inferred open-jaw rotation or proportional measurements as exact image measurements.

Taxonomy varies by source date. The cited agency and older biomechanics papers use *M. salmoides* for largemouth bass. A [2022 phylogenomics revision](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-11743-2) proposes *M. nigricans* for largemouth and reserves *M. salmoides* for Florida bass; common-name and photographed jaw form here remain the intended C1 reference. This nomenclature change does not justify blending in smallmouth bass or generic predatory-fish heads.
