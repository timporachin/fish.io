## Observed modeling landmarks

- **Full side silhouette:** In stock 381475146, the left-facing fish begins near x=20, the eye centers near x=150, the gill cover ends near x=430, and the visible tail reaches the right edge near x=1600. Thus the head occupies about one quarter of visible total length, and the eye is roughly 8% of that length behind the snout. The back ascends sharply behind the head, stays broadly rounded above the trunk, then tapers through a narrow caudal peduncle. Approximate maximum trunk depth is 400 pixels versus about 170 pixels at the peduncle. These are perspective- and individual-dependent image landmarks, not species measurements.
- **Head and jaw:** The snout is blunt and rounded in front of the eye, without a long beak. The upper and lower lips are thick, with a broad dark seam when closed and a substantial fleshy lower jaw when slightly open. The lower jaw slopes back into a rounded cheek/maxilla plate under the eye, then into the operculum. The eye is circular, glossy and recessed into the head. Avoid a planar gill-line decal or a tiny pointed muzzle.
- **Gill cover and paired fin:** The cheek and operculum form several overlapping curved surfaces; the rearmost gill-cover rim sits just ahead of the translucent pectoral base. The pectoral fin starts low on the flank behind the gill cover and spreads into a thin, ribbed fan. Its outer edge is tapered and translucent, not a solid opaque triangle.
- **Front cross-section, head-on image:** The forehead arches across the head into a wide, rounded muzzle. The eyes are high and widely spaced, projecting slightly from both sides; the mouth/jaw mass is broad and deep beneath them, not a small horizontal slit. Both pectorals fan laterally from behind the cheek and show many thin radial rays. The body behind the head is considerably narrower in this perspective.
- **Fins in full side view:** The posterior soft dorsal rises behind the body midpoint to a tall rounded-triangle lobe, then drops steeply to the peduncle. The anterior spinous portion lies low or folded against the back in this photo, so it cannot be used to argue that a standing first dorsal is absent. The broad, thin-rayed pectoral fans backward from immediately behind the gill cover; a smaller pelvic fin lies directly beneath its rear portion. The anal fin is a distinct fan behind the ventral midpoint. The caudal fin spreads into a broad fan with no deep fork; its far edge is clipped, so the exact central notch is unresolved.
- **Body and skin:** The body is deepest a short distance behind the head and narrows progressively toward the peduncle. In the second side image, olive green on the back passes through gold-green scales to a pale gray-white ventral head. Scales are individually evident as small overlapping spots, especially dorsal to the lateral band. The dark flank mark is irregular and broken into patches, with several short vertical drops; it is not a clean painted stripe. The full-profile image has a strong cyan-green cast from underwater lighting, not a literal material swatch.

The photographs support the major proportions, fin placement and surface treatment. They do not resolve exact spine and ray counts or the precise caudal notch, because the full-profile fish folds its anterior dorsal and the tail edge is slightly clipped.
